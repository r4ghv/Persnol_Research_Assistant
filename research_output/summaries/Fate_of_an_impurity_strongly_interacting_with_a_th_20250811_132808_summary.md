# Summary

- **Study Focus**: Investigated the behavior of mobile impurities in a thermal Bose gas, varying temperature and impurity-bath interaction strength.
- **Key Findings**:
  - Spectra of impurities narrow with increasing temperature for strong interactions, contrary to expectations.
  - Impurity energy shift is suppressed compared to a quasipure Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC).
  - Near the critical temperature for condensation, many-body effects are significant, with the system transitioning to classical Boltzmann-gas behavior only in nondegenerate conditions.
- **Theoretical Framework**: Key spectral features align with the ideal Bose polaron theory, indicating the applicability of polaron quasiparticle concepts to the system.
- **Experimental Setup**: Utilized a gas of 39K atoms in an optical box trap, with tunable parameters such as temperature and density, and employed rf injection spectroscopy to map impurity spectra.
- **Comparative Analysis**: Results contrasted with those from a quasipure BEC, highlighting differences in spectral shifts and widths influenced by temperature and interaction strength.
- **Significance**: Provides insights into impurity dynamics in quantum gases, contributing to the understanding of many-body physics and the behavior of strongly interacting bosonic systems.
