# Summary

- **Focus on Self-Normalized Concentration**: The study investigates the self-normalized concentration of vector-valued stochastic processes, particularly for sub-$\psi$ processes, which include various well-known distributions.

- **Generalization of Existing Bounds**: The results extend and generalize the significant bound established by Abbasi-Yadkori et al. (2011), bridging the gap between determinant-based and condition number-based bounds.

- **Bernstein Inequalities**: The paper proves a Bernstein inequality applicable to random vectors that meet a moment condition, which is broader than merely boundedness.

- **First Dimension-Free Empirical Bernstein Inequality**: It introduces the first dimension-free, self-normalized empirical Bernstein inequality, enhancing the theoretical framework for empirical processes.

- **Variational Approach**: The methodologies employed are rooted in the variational (PAC-Bayes) approach to concentration, which has been effective in analyzing random vectors and matrices.

- **Practical Relevance**: The findings have implications for various applications, including system identification, control of dynamic systems, and sequential inference in time-series, particularly in scenarios involving ill-conditioned matrices.

- **Comprehensive Framework**: The work contributes to a unified understanding of self-normalized and non-self-normalized bounds, enhancing the theoretical landscape of stochastic processes.
