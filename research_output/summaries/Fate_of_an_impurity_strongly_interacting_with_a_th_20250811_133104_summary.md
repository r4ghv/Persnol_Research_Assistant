# Summary

- **Study Focus**: Investigated mobile impurities in a thermal Bose gas, examining the effects of temperature and impurity-bath interaction strength.
  
- **Key Findings**:
  - Spectra of impurities narrow with increasing temperature under strong interaction conditions.
  - Impurity energy shift is suppressed compared to a quasipure Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC).
  - Near the critical temperature for condensation, many-body effects are significant, with the system approaching classical Boltzmann-gas behavior only in a nondegenerate bath.

- **Theoretical Framework**: Results align with the ideal Bose polaron theory, indicating that impurities can be treated as polaron quasiparticles.

- **Experimental Setup**: Used a gas of 39K atoms in an optical box trap, with tunable parameters such as temperature and density, and employed rf injection spectroscopy to measure impurity spectra.

- **Observations**:
  - Spectral shifts and widths are influenced by the energy scale related to the impurity and bath atom mass.
  - The attractive spectral branch shows continuity across resonance, and spectral width exhibits asymmetry based on interaction parameters.

- **Comparative Analysis**: Findings contrasted with previous studies on fermionic systems and highlighted the unique challenges posed by bosonic impurities, particularly regarding stability and three-body losses.

- **Implications**: Results contribute to understanding many-body physics in quantum gases and the behavior of impurities in thermal environments.
