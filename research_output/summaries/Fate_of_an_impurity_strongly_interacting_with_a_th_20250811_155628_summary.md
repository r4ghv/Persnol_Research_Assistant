# Summary

- **Research Focus**: Investigated the behavior of mobile impurities in a thermal Bose gas, examining the effects of temperature and impurity-bath interaction strength.
- **Key Findings**:
  - Spectra of impurities narrow with increasing temperature under strong interaction conditions.
  - Impurity energy shift is suppressed compared to a quasipure Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC).
  - Near critical temperature for condensation, many-body effects significantly influence the system.
  - In nondegenerate baths, the system approaches classical Boltzmann-gas behavior.
- **Theoretical Framework**: Results align with the ideal Bose polaron theory, demonstrating the applicability of this model in describing impurity behavior in a bosonic bath.
- **Experimental Methodology**: Employed rf injection spectroscopy to map impurity spectra across varying temperatures and densities, utilizing a box-trapped Bose gas of 39K atoms.
- **Comparative Analysis**: Findings contrasted with previous studies on strongly attractive interactions, highlighting unique behaviors in the strongly interacting bosonic regime.
- **Significance**: Contributes to understanding impurity dynamics in quantum gases, with implications for many-body physics and condensed matter research.
