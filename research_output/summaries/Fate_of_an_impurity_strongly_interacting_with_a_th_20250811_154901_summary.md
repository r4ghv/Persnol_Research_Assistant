# Summary

- **Study Focus**: Investigated the behavior of mobile impurities in a thermal Bose gas, analyzing the effects of temperature and impurity-bath interaction strength.
  
- **Key Findings**:
  - Spectra of impurities narrow with increasing temperature for strong interactions, contrasting with expectations.
  - Impurity energy shift is suppressed compared to a quasipure Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC).
  - Near the critical temperature for condensation, many-body effects are significant, with the system approaching classical Boltzmann-gas behavior only in a nondegenerate bath.

- **Theoretical Framework**: Results align with the ideal Bose polaron theory, which describes impurities as quasiparticles influenced by the excitations of the bosonic bath.

- **Experimental Setup**: Utilized a gas of 39K atoms in an optical box trap, with tunable parameters including temperature and impurity-bath interaction strength via Feshbach resonances.

- **Spectral Analysis**: Identified three key signatures in the impurity spectrum:
  1. Spectral shifts and widths determined by the energy scale related to the impurity and bath atom mass.
  2. Attractive spectral branch behavior across resonance.
  3. Asymmetry in spectral width as a function of interaction parameter.

- **Comparison to Previous Work**: Results extend understanding of impurity physics in thermal bosonic systems, addressing gaps in previous experiments that focused on strong attractive interactions. 

- **Implications**: Findings contribute to the broader understanding of many-body physics and the behavior of impurities in quantum gases, with potential applications in condensed matter physics and related fields.
